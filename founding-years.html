Founding Years

How Anna Regina Multilateral School was established and grew to serve the Essequibo Coast community.

Back to History

Anna Regina Multilateral School was built to serve a community and a purpose. From its very first classrooms on the Essequibo Coast, A.R.M.S. set out to give every student a real pathway forward, shaping lives across Region 2 for generations.

A School for the Essequibo Coast

A.R.M.S. was established in 1961 to serve the growing secondary education needs of the Essequibo Coast community. Named after the town of Anna Regina in Region 2 (Pomeroon-Supenaam), the school was designed as a multilateral institution, offering both traditional academic and technical/vocational streams to give every student a pathway to success.

The Multilateral Model

The multilateral format was a deliberate choice, reflecting Guyana's commitment to providing diverse educational pathways. Students at A.R.M.S. could pursue CSEC academic subjects alongside technical and vocational qualifications, ensuring graduates were prepared for both higher education and the world of work. This model set A.R.M.S. apart and made it a cornerstone of community development on the coast.

Early Growth

In its early years, A.R.M.S. attracted students from across the Essequibo Coast and surrounding villages. Word spread quickly of the school's commitment to discipline, academic rigour, and community values. Student enrolment grew steadily, and the first generation of A.R.M.S. graduates went on to the University of Guyana and careers across the country, establishing a proud tradition of achievement.

Building a Culture

From the beginning, A.R.M.S. fostered a strong school culture, including a prefect system, house competitions, cultural events tied to Guyana's national calendar including Mashramani, and early clubs that laid the foundation for today's vibrant extracurricular programme. The values instilled in those first classrooms still echo through every graduating class.
